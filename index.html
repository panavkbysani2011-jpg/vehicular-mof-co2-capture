System Architecture

Coordinated Three-Phase Physical Process

How tailpipe exhaust is ducted through cooperative chemisorptive sorbents, regenerated using thermal waste heat, and sequestered into stable solid minerals.

Phase 1

Packed MOF Chemisorption

Exhaust gas containing 5% to 15% CO2 flows through a packed bed of diamine-appended Mg-MOF-74 (m-2-m-Mg-dobpdc). Cooperative amine insertion creates step-shaped adsorption isotherms, achieving high capacity even at low partial pressures.

Sorbent Matrix: m-2-m-Mg-dobpdc
Nominal Capacity: 0.25 kg CO2 / kg MOF
Uptake Rate: k = 0.04 min^-1
Phase 2

Waste Heat Regeneration

When the sorbent reaches target saturation, hot exhaust gas (300 to 600 deg C) is diverted through a coaxial heat exchanger jacket. Sorbent temperature rises to 85 - 110 deg C, triggering endothermic carbamate bond cleavage without drawing electrical alternator power.

Exchanger Geometry: Coaxial Shell & Tube
Release Window: 85 to 110 deg C
Regeneration Time: < 55 seconds (at 15 kW)
Phase 3

Aqueous Calcite Mineralization

Desorbed CO2 gas bubbles directly into an unpressurized 30 L limewater tank containing a 20 wt% Ca(OH)2 slurry. Instantaneous precipitation locks carbon dioxide into dense, insoluble solid calcium carbonate (calcite), safely stored without pressurized cylinders.

Slurry Chemistry: 20 wt% Ca(OH)2 Slurry
Calcite Yield: 2.274 kg CaCO3 / kg CO2
Storage Pressure: 0.0 bar (Ambient Tank)

Governing Formulations

Mathematical Model and Algorithm

Exact analytical equations and kinetic derivations governing dynamic mass transfer, thermal carbamate cleavage, and mineralization stoichiometry.

1. Linear Driving Force Adsorption Kinetics

First-order mass transfer approach toward equilibrium capacity over driving cycle time.

$$q(t) = q_{\text{eq}} - \left(q_{\text{eq}} - q_{\text{start}}\right) \cdot e^{-k \cdot t}$$
Where: $q_{\text{eq}} = m_{\text{MOF}} \cdot Q_{\text{max}} \cdot \left(\frac{C_{\text{CO}_2}}{C_{\text{ref}}}\right)$, $k = 0.04\text{ min}^{-1}$, $Q_{\text{max}} = 0.25\text{ kg/kg}$.

2. Gibbs-Derived Sigmoidal Desorption

Logistic release fraction driven by cooperative carbamate polymer chain collapse.

$$\sigma(T) = \frac{1}{1 + \exp\left[-k_\sigma \cdot \left(T - T_m\right)\right]}$$
Where: $T_m = 90.0\text{ }^\circ\text{C}$ (transition midpoint), $k_\sigma = 0.08\text{ }^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$, $m_{\text{released}} = q(t) \cdot \sigma(T)$.

3. Regeneration Thermal Duty Balance

Sensible bed heating combined with molar carbamate cleavage enthalpy.

$$Q_{\text{total}} = \left[m_{\text{MOF}} \cdot c_{\text{MOF}} \cdot \left(T - T_0\right)\right] + \left(\frac{q(t)}{M_{\text{CO}_2}} \cdot \Delta H_{\text{des}}\right)$$
Where: $c_{\text{MOF}} = 0.80\text{ kJ/(kg}\cdot\text{K)}$, $\Delta H_{\text{des}} = 45.0\text{ kJ/mol}$, $M_{\text{CO}_2} = 44.01\text{ g/mol}$.

4. Stoichiometric Calcite Precipitation

Aqueous neutralization forming solid, inert calcium carbonate.

$$m_{\text{CaCO}_3} = m_{\text{released}} \cdot \left(\frac{M_{\text{CaCO}_3}}{M_{\text{CO}_2}}\right) \approx 2.274 \cdot m_{\text{released}}$$
Where: $M_{\text{CaCO}_3} = 100.09\text{ g/mol}$, $M_{\text{Ca(OH)}_2} = 74.09\text{ g/mol}$, $K_{\text{sp}} = 3.3 \times 10^{-9}$.
